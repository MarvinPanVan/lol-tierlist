          How the tier list is made

          Where the numbers come from, how they are combined into one score and tier, how far the big stat sites can be trusted, and what a tier list cannot tell you.

          Same numbers everywhere, very different grades

          At the same rank every site reads the same Riot match data, so their win rates nearly match. Their letter grades do not. Below is how closely each site's tiers follow winning versus popularity, as rank correlations across about 200 champion-role pairs at Diamond+.

          Last column: how closely the site's win rates match OP.GG at the same rank (1 = identical). Mobalytics' expert list has no win rates.

          U.GG and Blitz are the sites whose letters you can take at face value. dpm.lol mixes in popularity, and Mobalytics' expert list and METAsrc mostly tell you what people pick: both put Kai'Sa, Lee Sin, Lulu and Nautilus in their top tier, and all four win less than the average pick in their role. So when a champion's note says sites disagree, it only counts sites at 0.3 or more in the first column.

          How the score works

          • Per dataset: win rate minus that dataset's average for the role, in percentage points.
          • Sample size: each number is pulled toward zero by how noisy it is. Game counts come from OP.GG's sample totals and U.GG. How hard to pull is fitted from the data for every dataset and role, not guessed.
          • One-tricks: a small penalty under 2% pick rate, because mostly mains play those champions and their win rate flatters the champion.
          • Every list is one rank and one region. Ranks are Emerald+, Diamond+ and Master+, and each counts every game at that rank or higher. All 12 combinations are scored the same way and use the same tier cutoffs.
          • All regions: every server together at the chosen rank, each at its real share of games. At Emerald+ that is OP.GG's global numbers; at Diamond+ OP.GG, U.GG, Blitz, dpm.lol and League of Graphs (half weight) averaged; at Master+ OP.GG and U.GG. EUW, KR and NA each have their own list too.
          • One region: that region's OP.GG numbers, pulled toward the all-regions value of the same rank instead of toward zero. A region only moves away from the global value as far as its own games carry it, because regions rarely differ by more than about half a point while a few hundred games swing by two. Tested on 20 Nov against 26.22's region numbers (other games): about 4 to 7 times less error than reading each region on its own, and the ± ranges hold. Early in a patch, and at Master+, the region lists therefore look a lot like the all-regions list.
          • Tiers follow the same rules for every list, no hand edits. Roles spread out very differently (support runs from about +2 to −7, top only from +2 to −3), so S to D are cut in steps of each role's own spread: S from 1 spread above average and above average in every region (EUW, KR and NA, each read as described above), A from 0.45, B from 0, C from −0.6, D below. The spread is the pick-weighted standard deviation of the regular picks (1.5%+ pick) in the all-regions list of that rank; the single-region lists use the same lines, so their fewer games can't make the lines tighter. Z needs +1.8 and at least 1.6 spreads above average (so wide roles don't collect outliers), +1.5 or better in every region and a 2%+ pick rate. Until 20 Nov these region checks used each region's raw numbers, which this early in a patch swing by ±2 from luck alone; that decided 26 of 38 S and Z calls. Picks under 1.5% get their letter by the same lines. If the role is the champion's main one (where it is picked most in Global Emerald+), it stays in the list like any other pick, for example Taric support. Off-role picks under 1.5% sit in their own row at the bottom, best first.
          • Last patch is the starting point for champions the new patch did not touch, so early-patch numbers don't jump around. This uses OP.GG's global numbers at Emerald+, Diamond+ and Master+. How far a champion can move between patches is fitted from the data once per rank over all roles: about 0.2 points at Emerald+, 0.3 at Diamond+ and 0.4 at Master+. Checked on 26.22 to 26.23 with different games: last patch predicts this patch with an error of 0.06, 0.16 and 0.40, against about 1.8 without it.
          • The ± next to a score is a rough 90% range. Arrows show the change in Global Emerald+ since last patch. For champions the patch did not touch that change is damped on purpose, because last patch is their starting point.
          • A move only gets a reason when there is a fact for it: the change in the patch notes (Summoner's Rift only, not the Classic mode or ARAM), a changed item in its most common OP.GG builds (core items and boots, not starter items), a big shift in pick rate, or a note that the move is too small to be sure. No guessed explanations.
          • Matchups come from OP.GG's full matchup lists (Emerald+, and Diamond+ for the Diamond+ and Master+ lists). A matchup's result moves almost point for point with how strong the opponent is in general, so a plain list of the lowest win rates is mostly just the strongest champions of the role. Each matchup is therefore compared with what the champion should win against an opponent that strong (its own win rate minus the opponent's lead over 50%), then pulled toward that by sample size like the scores, so a lucky 150-game matchup can't top the list. Grey means the gap could still be luck. Bot lane duos work the same way with the partner's strength. The counter-pick finder is different on purpose: when you pick into someone, a pick's general strength counts too.
          • The build card shows what OP.GG players actually take and how often. Tapping a row shows the other options, and "more vs" when players clearly switch against certain lane opponents (OP.GG's builds for the 6 most played matchups). Items get no win rates: whoever finishes items is often ahead already. For most options there is no reliable "when" yet, because items and boots depend on the whole enemy team; our own Riot match sample records builds and enemy teams from now on to answer that.
          • Win rate by game length is OP.GG's, per 5-minute bucket. OP.GG gives no game counts per bucket, so they are estimated from the game lengths in our own Riot match sample, and the card only says a champion wins more in short or long games when the gap is 2+ points and bigger than that noise.

          What it cannot tell you

          • Your champion pool. A one-trick on a B-tier pick beats a first-timer on a Z.
          • Draft. Matchups and builds show what happens on average, not what your team needs. Mage bots and Kog'Maw need the right support.
          • Chinese servers. Tencent's data lives in its apps and is not public.
          • Anything after 26.23. Patch 26.24 comes on 9 Dec, and its changes are not out yet.
          • Small samples early in the patch. This list was read on 20 Nov, two and a half days after 26.23 went live: about 400,000 Emerald+ games, but only about 7,000 Master+ games in NA. The ± ranges show it, and the list gets read again around 26 Nov.
          • Game counts for Blitz and League of Graphs. They don't show them, so OP.GG Diamond+ counts stand in.
          • How good the players are on the champion. That would need per-player data from the Riot API.
